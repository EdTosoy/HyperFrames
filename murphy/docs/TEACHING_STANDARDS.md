# Teaching standards

This is the authoritative progressive teaching policy for the whole series. Treat
the units as one continuous course: **Introduce → Explain → Demonstrate → Practice
→ Reinforce → Build upon**. Apply this cycle within the existing six production
phases in the [series bible](SERIES_BIBLE.md); it does not replace their scene order.
Prioritize understanding over arbitrary video duration or engagement metrics.

Define one measurable learning objective before writing. State what the learner
should recognize, choose or say by the end. Choose A1/A2 vocabulary and a familiar
situation so vocabulary does not hide the grammar.

Write an original script and examples from the objective. Consult private textbook
references for the concept, then set them aside before drafting. Do not paraphrase
a page line by line, copy an exercise set or trace an illustration. Record unit
metadata for navigation only; no textbook passages belong in the asset manifest or
published frames. See [asset policy](ASSET_POLICY.md).

Check every rule for accuracy and scope. Distinguish a useful beginner pattern
from an absolute claim. Verify exceptions/ambiguities against an appropriate
grammar reference during lesson development; Phase 1 makes no curriculum accuracy
claims. Have a person review explanations, spelling, agreement, naturalness,
answer keys and the objective.

Show meaning and form together. Highlight the words being explained; use labels
or position as well as color. Introduce only relevant terminology. Avoid competing
text while the speaker explains a different idea. Captions must match the approved
recorded delivery, including meaningful corrections.

Practice must test the objective and have an unambiguous answer. After the full
question and choices are displayed and any prompt caption finishes, allow five
seconds to think. Keep the question and choices visible throughout; use a subtle
progress bar that runs for the full pause. Reveal the correct answer only after
the pause, together with a simple explanation of why it is correct. Keep answer
text, explanations, captions and correctness cues hidden during the pause. Apply
this timing to current silent lessons and future guided practice; the historical
recorded draft retains its audio alignment until it is revised with the recording.
The recap should help the learner use the skill. Record review through the production
lifecycle and release evidence.

## Plan from prior learning

Consult [learning progression](LEARNING_PROGRESSION.md) before drafting. Use the
current unit's objectives and confirmed earlier teaching to distinguish:

- **New knowledge:** a concept, term, form or use not yet taught in an approved
  earlier lesson, or a new extension of something familiar. Introduce its meaning,
  explain it simply, demonstrate it with original examples and guide practice.
- **Prerequisite knowledge:** earlier teaching needed to understand this unit.
  Retrieve it with a brief reminder where needed, then build on it. An earlier
  mention or a planned explanation is not evidence that it was taught.

Follow the curriculum's progression and add complexity gradually. If prior
coverage is missing or uncertain, check the approved lesson rather than assuming
the unit number proves coverage. Supply the minimum explanation needed here and
record the gap; do not invent earlier teaching or future unit content.

## Explain terms and connect lessons

Explain an unfamiliar grammar term in plain English at its first teaching use,
next to an example that shows what it means. Use only terms needed for the current
objective; avoid advanced labels and definitions that add no beginner value.
Record which terms were actually explained, not merely displayed.

Later, reuse familiar language and visual anchors. When helpful, explicitly name
the earlier idea and show how today's form, meaning or use extends or differs from
it. A unit reference can help navigation, but must not replace the explanation
needed to follow the current example.

## Remind, review and reinforce

Explaining something once does not guarantee recall. Give a short plain-language
reminder, worked example or retrieval prompt when a prerequisite is essential,
easily forgotten or likely to be confused with the new concept. Put it at the point
of need so someone who missed the earlier lesson can still follow the video.

Avoid repeating a full earlier explanation by default. Review is welcome when it
has a teaching purpose: retrieve a useful pattern, contrast a likely confusion or
combine old and new knowledge in guided practice. Expand the reminder if a brief
cue leaves the current lesson unclear; there is no fixed time limit or ban on
re-explanation. Keep most attention on the current unit's objective.

Use original examples and meaningful visuals to connect meaning with form. Follow
practice with an explanatory answer and recap; reinforce earlier knowledge through
its use in the new context rather than automatically replaying its introduction.

## Defer deliberately and anticipate confusion

Keep explanations accurate within the unit's scope. If a related concept belongs
later, state only the boundary needed now, without teaching future rules or
terminology prematurely. Record the deferred concept and reason; name a later
unit only when verified. Do not turn a beginner simplification into a false
absolute rule or rely on deferred knowledge to answer today's practice.

Before writing, identify likely beginner misunderstandings, such as confusing
meaning with word order, extending a pattern too broadly or mistaking a new use
for an earlier one. Address the relevant confusion with a clear contrast,
highlighted example or guided question and explain the answer. During the existing
human review, check accuracy, clarity, useful reminders, unnecessary repetition
and continuity with earlier teaching. Maintain the progression record through the
[production workflow](PRODUCTION_WORKFLOW.md), using existing approvals.
