## 128–144s · I, you, we and they

## 160–176s · I work → he works

English uses a different verb form with he, she and it in these positive statements. I work, but he works. The added s matches he; it does not mean several actions.

## 208–224s · One you still uses work

I means one speaker, but we still say I work, not I works. You work is correct for one listener or several. The s rule is for the he, she and it group.

## 224–240s · Most verbs: add s

For most verbs, add s with he, she or it. Work becomes works, read becomes reads, and play becomes plays.

## 240–256s · Watch, finish and pass

After endings such as ch, sh and ss in these verbs, add es. Watch becomes watches, finish becomes finishes, and pass becomes passes.

## 256–272s · Go → goes; do → does

## 272–288s · Have changes to has

## 288–304s · Study → studies

A, e, i, o and u are vowel letters; d and r here are consonants. Study ends in consonant plus y. Change y to ies: studies. Try becomes tries.

## 304–320s · Play keeps its y
