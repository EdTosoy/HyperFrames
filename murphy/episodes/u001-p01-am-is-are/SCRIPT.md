Why do we use three different words?

Am, is, and are are forms of the verb be in the present.
The word we choose depends on the subject.
With I, we use am.
With he, she, and it, we use is.
And with you, we, and they, we use are.

In everyday English, we often shorten these expressions.
I am becomes I'm.
She is becomes she's.
And they are becomes they're.
