### Scene 1 — Hook

Listen to these three sentences.
I am excited.
She is excited.
They are excited.
All three sentences describe the same feeling. So why do we use am, is, and are?

### Scene 2 — Understanding the meaning

First, let's understand what these words mean.
Am, is, and are are three forms of the same verb: be.
We use this verb to describe someone or something.
For example, how someone feels, who they are, or where they are.
In this lesson, we're using the present tense.
That means we're talking about something that's true now, or something that's generally true.
For example:
"She is happy." That's how she feels now.
"She is a teacher." That's something generally true about her.

### Scene 3 — Why does the verb change?

Now, here's the important part.
The word we use depends on the subject.
What's a subject?
It's the person or thing we're talking about.
When the subject is I, we use am.
"I am happy."
When the subject is he, she, or it, we use is.
"She is happy."
And when the subject is you, we, or they, we use are.
"They are happy."
So remember, the subject tells us which form to use.

### Scene 4 — Short forms

In everyday English, we often make these expressions shorter.
These are called contractions.
"I am" becomes "I'm."
"She is" becomes "She's."
And "They are" becomes "They're."
The meaning stays the same. We're simply using shorter forms.

### Scene 5 — Practice

Now, let's try one.
"My classmates \_\_\_ outside."
Should we use am, is, or are?
[Pause for three seconds. Do not read the blank aloud.]
The answer is are!
"My classmates are outside."
Why?
Because "my classmates" refers to more than one person.
We can replace it with they.
And we already know that "they" goes with "are".

### Scene 6 — Recap

Let's review.
I — am.
He, she, it — is.
You, we, they — are.
And that's how we choose between am, is, and are.
