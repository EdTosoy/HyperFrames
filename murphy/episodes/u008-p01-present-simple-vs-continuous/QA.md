## Educational scope and accuracy

The owner outline supplies complete-unit scope; no private textbook pages were
available locally. Meaning precedes formula retrieval. The same reader/book,
players/game, cook/kitchen and worker/workspace appear in current-activity and
routine views. Both descriptions can be true. A break within a current reading
project explicitly prevents the false rule that continuous must mean this exact
second. No future arrangements or later-unit tense rules are taught.

Questions and negatives retrieve Units 3–7 forms without repeating their full
lectures. Current non-viewing differs from occasional viewing; current non-driving
differs from a declared train commute. Knowledge, wants, needs, feelings, belief,
memory, preference and meaning introduce ordinary state uses. Like, love, want,
need, know, understand, remember, believe, prefer and mean are scoped as generally
simple in the meanings taught here; forget is presented in a repeated memory
habit. Other meanings/contexts can allow continuous forms: no absolute ban.
Having lunch is distinguished briefly from possession of a parked bicycle.

The Sunday-schedule correction is tied to the intended ordinary routine and
explicitly acknowledges that continuous wording can fit other contexts. Exercises
include semantic choices, both-true reasoning, state meanings, form repair and
around-now scope rather than only time-keyword spotting.

References: [British Council present continuous](https://learnenglishteens.britishcouncil.org/grammar/a1-a2-grammar/present-continuous)
for now/around-now and familiar forms; [British Council stative verbs](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/b1-b2/stative-verbs)
for ordinary state meanings and have. Advanced examples from those pages were not
imported. Cambridge's full comparison page returned HTTP 403; the accessible
official references and owner outline ground the explanations.
