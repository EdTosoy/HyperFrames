# Unit 2 — editable narration script v01

Silent draft. Owner recording pending. Timings and captions are provisional; bracketed pauses are not spoken.

### 00:00–00:24 · Tell or ask?

Welcome back. In Unit 1, we described people and things with am, is and are. She is ready tells us something. Is she ready asks us to check. Today we will build questions and answer them.

### 00:24–00:52 · Move be to the front

The subject is who or what we mean: here, she. In the statement, is follows she. In the standard question, is comes before she. The words keep their jobs, but their order changes. We also use a question mark.

### 00:52–01:14 · Watch the words move

Watch is move in front of she. She moves into the second position. Ready stays at the end. Now read the question: is she ready? Use this movement when you change a statement into a question.

### 01:14–01:36 · A longer subject

The bus is late becomes is the bus late? The subject is the whole phrase, the bus. Move is before that whole phrase. Keep the bus together.

### 01:36–01:58 · More than one person

Are your neighbours home? Your neighbours means they, so we use are. The subject can be several words. Put are before the whole subject, not inside it.

### 01:58–02:22 · Questions with am and is

With I, use am: am I late? With he or she, use is: is he ready? Is she here? We keep the same verb choice from Unit 1 and change its position.

### 02:22–02:46 · Questions with are

With you, we and they, use are. Are you ready? Are we late? Are they home? You takes are whether we speak to one listener or several.

### 02:46–03:04 · Ask about a thing

For a thing we call it, use is. Is it open? We ask about the door. A yes or no answer can tell us whether our idea is right.

### 03:04–03:29 · What asks for information

What asks for a thing or information. What is your favourite food? Rice. The answer names the food, rather than saying yes or no.

### 03:29–03:54 · Who asks for a person

Who asks for a person. Who is that person? My teacher. We want to know which person this is.

### 03:54–04:19 · Where asks for a place

Where asks for a place. Where are my glasses? On the table. Imagine you cannot find your glasses. You need their location.

### 04:19–04:44 · How asks for a condition or detail

How asks for a condition or detail. How old is your brother? Twelve. How old asks about age. How are you asks how you feel. The words after how help show which detail we want.

### 04:44–05:09 · Why asks for a reason

Why asks for a reason. Why are you worried? My bus is late. The reply gives a reason for the feeling.

### 05:09–05:34 · Put the question word first

Are you here asks for yes or no. Where are you asks for a place. Start with where, then are, then you. The question word comes first, and be still comes before the subject.

### 05:34–05:52 · What’s = What is

You already know that contractions join words. What’s means What is. What’s your name? It means what is your name.

### 05:52–06:10 · Who’s = Who is

You already know that contractions join words. Who’s means Who is. Who’s that? It means who is that.

### 06:10–06:28 · Where’s = Where is

You already know that contractions join words. Where’s means Where is. Where’s my bag? It means where is my bag.

### 06:28–06:46 · How’s = How is

You already know that contractions join words. How’s means How is. How’s your sister? It means how is your sister.

### 06:46–07:14 · You in the question → I in the reply

Maya asks Omar: are you a student? Omar answers: yes, I am. Maya calls Omar you. Omar calls himself I. The person has not changed; the speaker has. With I, he uses am.

### 07:14–07:44 · Positive short answers

A short answer avoids repeating the whole description. Yes, I am means yes, I am a student. Say yes, he is; yes, she is; or yes, it is. With you, we and they, say yes, you are; yes, we are; yes, they are.

### 07:44–08:14 · Negative short answers

For no, keep the negative. No, I’m not. No, he isn’t; no, she isn’t; no, it isn’t. With you, we and they, use aren’t: no, you aren’t; no, we aren’t; no, they aren’t.

### 08:14–08:36 · Two natural negative answers

No, she isn’t and no, she’s not mean the same thing. Both are natural. The negative word not is still there. The short form changes, but the meaning stays the same.

### 08:36–08:58 · With we, too

No, we aren’t and no, we’re not are both natural. You and they can follow the same two patterns: you aren’t or you’re not; they aren’t or they’re not.

### 08:58–09:23 · Keep the verb at the end

For a positive short answer, keep the full verb at the end. Say yes, I am. Yes, I’m sounds unfinished on its own. You can say I’m ready when the description follows, but our short answer ends with am.

### 09:23–09:45 · She is, not she’s

The same idea applies to is. Say yes, she is, not yes, she’s on its own. With are, say yes, they are. Negative answers can use contractions because not is part of the answer.

### 09:45–10:10 · The standard question pattern

You may hear you are ready with a questioning voice, for example to check surprising news. For the standard neutral question we are learning, put are first: are you ready?

### 10:10–10:30 · After where, keep be

Start with where, but do not forget the next step. Put are before you: where are you? The question word does not replace the verb.

### 10:30–10:52 · Match the whole subject

Your parents means they: more than one person. Use are, not is. Are your parents home? Choose the verb for the whole subject, even when it is not a pronoun.

### 10:52–11:15 · Your turn

Change: They are home. [Pause five seconds.] Are they home?. Move are before they; keep the place at the end.

### 11:15–11:38 · Your turn

\_\_\_ I early? [Pause five seconds.] Am. I takes am. In a question, am comes first.

### 11:38–12:01 · Your turn

\_\_\_ your sister ready? [Pause five seconds.] Is. Your sister means she, so use is before the subject.

### 12:01–12:24 · Your turn

\_\_\_ your friends here? [Pause five seconds.] Are. Your friends means they; use are.

### 12:24–12:47 · Your turn

Arrange: you / are / where [Pause five seconds.] Where are you?. Where comes first, then are, then the subject you.

### 12:47–13:10 · Your turn

Ask for a place: \_\_\_ is my bag? [Pause five seconds.] Where. Where asks for location, so the reply names a place.

### 13:10–13:33 · Your turn

Ask for a reason: \_\_\_ are you sad? [Pause five seconds.] Why. Why asks for the reason for a feeling.

### 13:33–13:56 · Your turn

Omar asks you: Are you ready? Say yes. [Pause five seconds.] Yes, I am.. You speak about yourself as I; keep the full am.

### 13:56–14:19 · Your turn

Is she at home? Say no. [Pause five seconds.] No, she isn’t.. She takes is. No, she’s not is also natural.

### 14:19–14:49 · Ask, then answer

Put am, is or are before the subject in a standard question. Put a question word first when you want information. Choose be to match the subject. Use full verbs in positive short answers, and keep not in negative ones. Ask someone near you: are you ready? Then answer for yourself: yes, I am, or no, I’m not.
