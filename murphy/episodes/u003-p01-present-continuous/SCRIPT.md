## 18–36s · Familiar be, a new use

In Unit 1, she is happy describes a feeling. She is cooking describes an action in progress. We keep is and add the action word with ing.

## 36–54s · Present continuous

We call this the present continuous. Present refers to now in this lesson. Continuous means the action is in progress. Her message is not finished. She is writing as we speak.

[Visual: visual-context; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 54–72s · Look for action in progress

Look outside. Drops are falling past the window. It is raining. The rain is happening now. The present continuous is one way to describe the present, not the only way.

## 72–90s · Both parts matter

Use a subject, am, is or are, and an action word ending in ing. In she is reading, reading names the action. Is and reading work together to show an action in progress.

[Visual: sentence-builder; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 90–108s · Keep am in the sentence

I studying leaves out am in a standard full sentence. Say I am studying. Adding ing alone is not enough. Choose am, is or are to match the subject, just as in Unit 1.

## 108–126s · I → am

I am studying. The action is happening now. I’m studying. The contraction has the same meaning. Keep both be and the ing action when you shorten the sentence.

## 144–162s · You / we / they → are

## 162–180s · He is running

Watch his legs move through a stride. He is running. Is matches he; running tells us the action. Now we will change the situation, not just the words.

## 180–198s · Now he is standing

He is standing still now. He is not running. The running action is not happening. Not makes the continuous sentence negative.

[Visual: visual-context; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 198–216s · Put not after be

You know where not goes from Unit 1. I am not sleeping. Keep am, then not, then sleeping. The action still ends in ing in a negative sentence.

[Visual: sentence-builder; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 216–234s · Short negative forms

He is not working becomes he isn’t working. She isn’t dancing. They aren’t waiting. I’m not sleeping uses am not. Do not say I amn’t sleeping.

[Visual: phrase-focus; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 234–252s · Most verbs: add ing

For many verbs, keep the original letters and add ing. Read becomes reading. Play becomes playing. Work becomes working. The ending works with am, is or are to describe an action in progress.

[Visual: phrase-focus; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 252–270s · Remove silent e

In make, the final e is silent. Remove that e before adding ing: make becomes making. Write becomes writing and dance becomes dancing. These follow the same pattern.

[Visual: phrase-focus; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 270–288s · Double the last consonant

Run has one spoken beat, a short vowel sound, and one final consonant. Double n before ing: running. Sit becomes sitting and swim becomes swimming. The spelling and sound pattern matters; not every final consonant doubles.

[Visual: phrase-focus; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 288–306s · Check the pattern

Work ends with two consonant letters, r and k: working, not workking. Play keeps its y: playing. Do not double w, x or y for this pattern. Longer words depend on the stressed beat too. Learn these short examples first.

[Visual: phrase-focus; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 306–324s · Lie → lying

Here lie means rest with your body flat. For lie, change ie to y, then add ing: lying. She is lying on the bed. Keep is before lying.

[Visual: phrase-focus; seekable movement and progressive emphasis follow the displayed example.]

## 324–342s · An action includes NOW
